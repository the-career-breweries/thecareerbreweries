# 5.4.1 Importance of cybersecurity in aviation

* **The Threat Landscape:** Airlines handle massive amounts of sensitive data (passports, credit cards, flight paths).
* **The Human Element:** 90% of successful cyber attacks occur because an employee made a mistake, not because of hacking software.
* **Consequences:** Data breaches lead to severe financial penalties (GDPR), loss of reputation, and operational shutdowns.

---

# 5.4.2 Recognizing phishing emails and scams

* **Phishing:** Fraudulent emails designed to trick you into revealing passwords or clicking malicious links.
* **Red Flags:**
  1. Urgent, threatening language ("Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!").
  2. Generic greetings ("Dear Customer").
  3. Mismatched URLs (Hovering over a link shows it goes to `apple-support-login.com` instead of `apple.com`).
  4. Unexpected attachments.

# 5.4.3 Creating and managing strong passwords

* **Length beats complexity:** `CorrectHorseBatteryStaple` is much harder for a computer to crack than `P@$$w0rd`.
* **Password Reuse:** NEVER use the same password for your airline login that you use for your personal Netflix account. If Netflix gets hacked, hackers will try that password everywhere.
* **Two-Factor Authentication (2FA):** Always enable 2FA. Even if a hacker steals your password, they cannot log in without the code sent to your phone.

---

# 5.4.4 Understanding malware, viruses, and antivirus

* **Malware:** Broad term for 'Malicious Software' (Viruses, Worms, Trojans).
* **Ransomware:** A specific, highly dangerous malware that encrypts all files on a computer and demands payment to unlock them.
* **Antivirus:** Always keep Windows Defender enabled and updated. Do not plug in random USB drives found in airports or parking lots (USB Drop attacks).

---

# 5.4.5 Safe browsing and downloading practices

* **HTTPS:** Look for the padlock icon in the browser address bar. Never enter passwords or credit cards on sites that say 'Not Secure'.
* **Downloading:** Only download software from official vendor websites. Never use third-party cracked software sites.
* **Public Wi-Fi:** Airport and coffee shop Wi-Fi networks are public. Do not check sensitive bank accounts without using a VPN (Virtual Private Network).

---

# 5.4.6 Hands-on: Security settings and password audit

* **Step 1:** Go to a safe website like `haveibeenpwned.com` to check if your personal email has been involved in a data breach.
* **Step 2:** Open Windows Security / Windows Defender settings and run a 'Quick Scan'.
* **Step 3:** Review your browser's saved passwords list and delete any weak/old ones.
* **Step 4:** Analyze 3 example 'Phishing' emails provided by the instructor and identify the red flags.
