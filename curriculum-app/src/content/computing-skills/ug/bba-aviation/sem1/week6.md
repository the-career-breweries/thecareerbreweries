# 🧠 Key Concept

- Tables Explained: Rows, Columns & Cells

- A table organises information into a grid of rows (horizontal) and columns (vertical). Each box where a row and column meet is called a cell, and holds one piece of data.

- Example: a 3-row, 3-column table

- MERGE vs SPLIT CELLS
- Merge cells joins two or more cells into one larger cell — often used to create a title row that spans the full width of a table.
- Split cells divides one cell into multiple smaller cells — the reverse of merging.

---

# 🧠 Key Concept

- Report Structure, Headers & Footers

- A well-structured report typically follows this order:

- HEADER vs FOOTER
- Header: text that repeats automatically at the top of every page — often a document title or chapter name.
- Footer: text that repeats automatically at the bottom of every page — often a page number or author name.
- Page numbers inserted through the header/footer tools update automatically as pages are added or removed, unlike numbers typed in manually.
