## Key Concepts
- **The 8 Parts of Speech**: The fundamental building blocks of the English language that determine how words function in meaning as well as grammatically within the sentence.
- **Types of Sentences**: The structural classification of sentences based on the number and type of clauses they contain: Simple, Compound, Complex, and Compound-Complex.
- **Tenses**: Grammatical forms that indicate the time of an action or event (past, present, future), allowing for accurate chronological storytelling and reporting.
- **Subject-Verb Agreement (SVA)**: The critical grammatical rule ensuring that singular subjects pair with singular verbs, and plural subjects pair with plural verbs, maintaining sentence clarity.


---

## Important Definitions
- **Noun**: A word used to identify any of a class of people, places, or things (e.g., Pilot, Airport, Happiness).
- **Pronoun**: A word that can function by itself as a noun phrase and that refers either to the participants in the discourse (e.g., I, you) or to someone or something mentioned elsewhere (e.g., she, it, this).
- **Verb**: A word used to describe an action, state, or occurrence (e.g., Fly, Communicate, Is).
- **Adjective**: A word or phrase naming an attribute, added to or grammatically related to a noun to modify or describe it (e.g., Clear, Professional, Huge).
- **Adverb**: A word or phrase that modifies or qualifies an adjective, verb, or other adverb, expressing a relation of place, time, circumstance, manner, cause, degree, etc. (e.g., Quickly, Very, Well).
- **Preposition**: A word governing, and usually preceding, a noun or pronoun and expressing a relation to another word (e.g., On, In, At).
- **Conjunction**: A word used to connect clauses or sentences or to coordinate words in the same clause (e.g., And, But, If).
- **Interjection**: An abrupt remark, made especially as an aside or interruption (e.g., Ah!, Wow!, Oh!).
- **Simple Sentence**: A sentence consisting of only one independent clause, with a single subject and predicate.
- **Compound Sentence**: A sentence containing two or more independent clauses joined by a coordinating conjunction (e.g., for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so).
- **Complex Sentence**: A sentence containing one independent clause and at least one dependent (subordinate) clause.
- **Compound-Complex Sentence**: A sentence having two or more independent clauses and one or more dependent clauses.
- **Subject-Verb Agreement**: The grammatical rule stating that the subject and verb must agree in number. A singular subject takes a singular verb, and a plural subject takes a plural verb.
- **Simple Present Tense**: Used to describe habits, unchanging situations, general truths, and fixed arrangements.
- **Present Continuous Tense**: Used to describe an action that is happening right now or in the near future.
- **Present Perfect Tense**: Used to describe an action that happened at an unspecified time in the past or began in the past and continues to the present.
- **Simple Past Tense**: Used to talk about a completed action in a time before now.
- **Simple Future Tense**: Used to talk about things that haven't happened yet.

## Mnemonic: FANBOYS
- F - For
- A - And
- N - Nor
- B - But
- O - Or
- Y - Yet
- S - So

## Knowledge Check (BTL)
- **Remember**: List the eight parts of speech.
- **Understand**: Explain the difference between a common noun and a proper noun.
- **Apply**: Identify the verbs in a given paragraph.
- **Analyze**: Break down a complex sentence into its subject, verb, and object.
- **Evaluate**: Critique a given text for grammatical accuracy.
- **Create**: Construct a paragraph using all eight parts of speech.
