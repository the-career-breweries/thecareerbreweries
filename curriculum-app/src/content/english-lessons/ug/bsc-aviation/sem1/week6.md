# Class 1: Fundamentals of Active Listening

**Lead-in:** Have you ever spoken to someone who was looking at their phone and saying "Uh-huh"? Were they hearing you, or listening to you?

*   **Hearing** is a biological function. It happens automatically. 
*   **Listening** is a psychological choice. It requires effort and focus.

**Gist Task:** We are going to listen to a 1-minute audio clip of a frustrated passenger. Listen only for the main emotion. Is it anger, fear, or confusion?

---

# Detailed Task: Paraphrasing

**Detailed Task:** Let's listen to the audio again. This time, note down the specific reason they are upset. 

**The Strategy (Paraphrasing):** Don't just parrot back what they said. Summarize the core issue and the emotion to confirm understanding.

*Passenger:* "I've been waiting for an hour! No one is telling me anything about my connecting flight to Dubai, and if I miss my meeting, I'm going to lose my job!"

*Active Listening Response:* "So if I understand correctly, you're incredibly anxious because the lack of updates is risking your crucial meeting in Dubai. Let me check the exact status for you."

---

# Follow-up: The S.O.F.T.E.N. Technique

Active listening isn't just about your ears; it's about your body language.

*   **S**mile (When appropriate)
*   **O**pen posture (No crossed arms)
*   **F**orward lean (Shows engagement)
*   **T**ouch (A professional handshake)
*   **E**ye Contact (Steady, break naturally)
*   **N**od (Validate you are listening)

# Review: Stress and Intonation

**Word Stress:** Emphasizing the correct syllable changes the meaning. 
*   *PRE-sent* (Noun: a gift) vs. *pre-SENT* (Verb: to give).

**Sentence Intonation:** 
*   Questions usually end with a rising pitch.
*   Statements end with a falling pitch.

# Class 3: Everyday Conversation Practice

**Lead-in:** Think about a time a stranger made small talk with you. What made it comfortable or awkward?

**Building Rapport:**
1.  **Professional Greetings:** "Good morning, Sir," not "Hey man."
2.  **Open vs. Closed Questions:**
    *   *Closed:* "Did you pack this bag yourself?" (Yes/No - good for security/facts).
    *   *Open:* "How has your journey been so far?" (Good for building rapport).

---

# Practice: Polite Disagreement

In professional settings, you will often need to disagree or say no.

**The Positive-Negative-Positive Sandwich:**
1. **Positive:** Validate their request. *"I completely understand you need to get home."*
2. **Negative (The Pivot):** State the firm boundary clearly. *"However, security regulations prevent us from allowing boarding without physical ID."*
3. **Positive (The Alternative):** Offer what you *can* do. *"What I can do is rebook you on the next flight at no charge."*

**Roleplay:** Disagree with a colleague who wants to skip a mandatory safety check.

# Class 4: Professional Speaking in Aviation

**Test 1 (5 mins):** 
Read this PA announcement to the class as fast and loud as you can: 
*"Ladies and gentlemen, Flight 202 is delayed due to weather. Please wait for further updates."*

**Teach:** In high-noise environments (terminals, cabins), speaking louder distorts the microphone.
*   **Modulation:** Vary your pitch to sound natural, not robotic.
*   **Pacing:** Speak 20% slower than your normal conversation speed.
*   **Clarity:** Enunciate consonants sharply.

---

# Test 2: The Safety Briefing

**The Task:** Deliver the following pre-flight briefing. Apply pacing, clarity, and modulation.

*"Good morning, ladies and gentlemen. Welcome aboard Flight 404 to Mumbai. Please ensure your seatbelts are securely fastened, tray tables are stowed, and window blinds are open. For your safety, we request your full attention as the cabin crew points out the emergency exits."*

*Peer Feedback: Did they sound authoritative yet welcoming? Did they speak too fast?*

# Class 5: Customer Service Role Play

**Pre-task:** We are combining Active Listening (S.O.F.T.E.N, Paraphrasing) with Professional Speaking (Tone, Pacing). 

**The H.E.A.R.T. Model for Crisis:**
*   **H**ear: Listen without interrupting.
*   **E**mpathize: Acknowledge feelings.
*   **A**pologize: Say sorry for the situation.
*   **R**esolve: Offer options.
*   **T**hank: Thank them for patience.

# Task Cycle: The Scenarios

Work with a partner. Take turns playing the Aviation Professional and the Passenger. You have 15 minutes.

**Scenario 1: The Frustrated Flyer**
A passenger is upset because their flight has been delayed by 3 hours.
*   *Professional:* Explain the delay calmly, use H.E.A.R.T, and offer a voucher.
*   *Passenger:* Express frustration and interrupt them.

**Scenario 2: The Lost Baggage**
A passenger's baggage is missing.
*   *Professional:* Guide them through the PIR process clearly.
*   *Passenger:* You are highly stressed and speaking very quickly.

# Exam Preparation: Listening & Speaking

**How you will be assessed in your Practical/Oral Exams:**

1.  **Clarity & Pronunciation (30%):** Are your words easily understood? Did you use correct word stress?
2.  **Active Listening (30%):** Did you answer the specific question asked, or did you give a memorized speech? Did you paraphrase effectively in roleplays?
3.  **Tone & Body Language (20%):** Did you maintain eye contact? Was your tone professional and empathetic?
4.  **Fluency (20%):** Can you maintain a conversation without excessive pauses or filler words (um, ah)?

**Top Exam Tip:** In a roleplay exam, if you don't hear the examiner clearly, *professionally ask them to repeat*. ("I apologize, could you please clarify that?") This demonstrates active listening and confidence!
